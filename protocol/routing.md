# Routing

Route identity is `harness@major × model_id × effort`. Public benchmark identity is only `model_id × effort`; never share runtime reliability, quota, dispatch-form, or adapter-trust evidence automatically across harnesses. The harness version counts by major only (`agy@1`, `codex@0`): point releases share trust and history, and the full version stays in the selection disclosure. `catalog/trust-baseline.yaml` ships maintainer-granted routes that read as proven on a fresh install; a local trust act overrides it.

Routing has two separate concerns: **qualification** (can this route run this work safely, now?) and **recommendation** (which qualifying route should run it?). Cost and preference are never qualification, except the explicit budget ceiling below.

## Qualification (every role)

Filter in this exact order: hard exclusions → adapter validity/trust → required capabilities → absolute role floor → task shape → quota safety.

**Each filter's input is derived, not caller-supplied (amendment v3, narrowed by v5/v6).** A candidate asserting its own `adapter_state`, `absolute_floor_pass`, or `local_reward` is routed exactly as if it had asserted nothing — `route()` ignores those fields and recomputes them:

- **Adapter validity/trust.** A query may **lower** adapter trust and may **never raise it**. An unresolved adapter-attributed critical failure quarantines a triple automatically, from recorded dispatch/outcome-label evidence, with no human action. Absent any explicit act the derived state is `valid-unverified`, never `proven`, no matter how many successful dispatches accumulated. Every upward transition of *adapter trust* (`quarantined` → `valid-unverified`, anything → `proven`) is an explicit recorded trust act carrying its own `actor_id`. See `protocol/adapters.md`. Learned eligibility (below) is a separate layer for executor/worker routes; it never writes or reads as a trust act and never lifts a quarantine.
- **Absolute role floor.** Evaluated against the candidate's pinned catalog row and the declared `roles.<role>.floor` in `config/config.default.yaml`. When a catalog field the floor needs is unknown, the floor fails closed and names the missing field; unknown never passes.
- **Quota.** The reserve defaults to 5%. Unknown quota is not unlimited. Exclude a candidate whose projected dispatch crosses reserve when another qualifying candidate exists. If all qualifying candidates cross reserve, return to the orchestrator for a smaller valid strategy, lower-cost route, or explicit user decision; do not lower capability floors.

**The recorded-override path is the only way past a derived gate.** An explicit override of adapter trust, the absolute floor, or the advisory/learned stage (7) is honoured only when a `RecordedOverride` exists carrying user attribution (`authorized_by: "user"`), the scope it covers, and an unexpired `expires_at`, and it then surfaces in `selection_disclosure`. A request that asserts `allow_unverified_override`/`allow_override` without a valid, unexpired recorded authorization is a hard stop (`status: "override_not_authorized"`). The normative artifact for the adapter-trust properties is `tests/test_trust_conformance.py` — see `docs/v3-runtime-contracts.md` §7. A user-declared route (`office dispatch --as`, `--route`) remains explicit user authority and wins where valid.

## Executor and worker recommendation (#300)

Executor and worker routes are recommended by `src/office/adaptive.py` over the qualifying set. For these roles only, this **supersedes** the deterministic stages that used to follow quota: the balanced 20% cheapest-money band, the preferred-seed winner chain, and the local-reward tie-break.

- **Stage 7, learned eligibility.** A route the learner has marked `learned-ineligible` is rejected; a `valid-unverified` executor route marked `learned-eligible` passes the trust stage with eligibility source `learned`. A gear that sets `allow_advisory_undercut: false` still holds routes to its seed chain here.
- **Stage 8, budget ceiling.** The only cost-based removal: a route whose expected cost to success exceeds `routing.adaptive.budget_ceiling_usd` (default 25; `null` disables) is rejected. The ceiling is absolute, so a very cheap route never makes every stronger route "too expensive".
- **Scoring.** Each qualifying route gets a success probability (a Beta posterior whose prior is the pinned benchmark, worth `prior_strength` pseudo-outcomes, so comparable local evidence takes over as it accumulates); an attempt cost (catalog price × effort token multiplier, or local spend when recorded); expected attempts and review rework from local evidence; expected **cost to success** and **time to success**; quota headroom (unknown scores below known-healthy); and user preference. Utility is the weighted sum of effectiveness, cost, speed, quota and preference (`routing.adaptive.weights.<cost_policy>`; preference ≤ 0.25 so it cannot steer every decision), minus a small soft-spread penalty for routes already planned in the same wave. Model × effort is the unit: efforts of one model compete independently, and more effort is never assumed better.
- **Slate.** The top three by utility, with no harness or family diversity forced, each with a reason, one strength and one weakness. When fewer qualify, fewer are returned.
- **Close calls.** Routes within `competitive_band` (default 5%, relative) of the best utility are a close call; a utility-weighted draw seeded by the routing seed (run, task, role, plan version, dispatch kind) picks among them. Same inputs and seed reproduce the draw. A route outside the band never wins by draw.
- **Exploration.** With probability `exploration.rate` (default 8%), under the rolling cap (at most 10% of the last 20 decisions) and the per-run cap (1), an under-tested qualifying route (local sample below `min_samples_mature`) within `margin` utility of the primary and at most 125% of its expected cost to success becomes the primary. It is recorded as exploration.

The planner owns the primary and two fallbacks. By default it accepts the slate. In PLAN.md it may name `route: <primary>, <fallback>, <fallback>` (any qualifying route, not only the displayed three) with `route_why:`. A primary outside the close-call band without a concrete `route_why` is ignored and the ranked slate stands, with a notice. Dispatch refreshes live evidence by routing again, then runs the first planned route that still qualifies, in recorded order, and records why each earlier one was skipped. It never runs an unplanned route: when every planned route fails, dispatch returns `slate_exhausted`, and `office dispatch <task> --reroute` routes from current evidence. A rerun keeps its original route. Fresh evidence that the route cannot run now (trust, quota crossing reserve, learned ineligibility) refuses it, and `office rerun <task> --fresh --reroute` routes again.

Planner and reviewer routes keep the legacy order until their own authority problem is redesigned: advisory anchor by `preferred_seed` chain → cost (`balanced` keeps candidates within `cost_policy.balanced_money_band_percent` of the cheapest money estimate, then prefers lower quota burn, lower wall clock, stronger local reward; also `money_saver`, `quota_saver`) → local tie-break.

## Disclosure and audit

Every successful route emits `selection_disclosure`: role, canonical `model_id`, exact `invocation_model_id` when exposed by the harness binding, effort, harness/version, selected triple, and a concise reason derived from the decisive evidence. Executor/worker disclosures add the compact slate. This object is durable handoff data.

Each executor/worker decision (plan preview, dispatch, reroute) appends one `route_audit` row whose `disclosure_json` follows `schemas/routing-decision.schema.json`. It holds the qualifying candidates with every score component and its evidence (benchmark prior and authority, local sample, attribution counts), the numeric order, the slate, the seed and draw, exploration, spread, the planner's choice and override reason, the dispatched route and any fallback taken, the evidence digest, and the policy and learner versions. `decision_hash` covers the slate, the scoring inputs, the draw and exploration, and the evidence digest. Pre-#300 single-route records stay readable through `office inspect route <task>`.

CLI disclosure is the Inline Slate (plan diagram, `office inspect route`). The evidence matrix is under `office inspect route`, and `--json` carries the complete audit record.

## Per-task descriptors and calibrated model fit (#415)

PLAN.md tasks may declare optional `domain`, `work`, `modality`, `quality`, `bounds`, `task_size`, `estimated_input_tokens`, and `estimated_output_tokens` fields; none is inferred from a model brand. The descriptor is validated per task, persisted in SQLite, snapshotted on each dispatch, and visible in route audits. Absent descriptors are neutral and preserve legacy routing. See [the research and evidence contract](../docs/routing-task-descriptors-415.md).

Specialized public scores are accepted only as catalog `task_benchmarks` records with a benchmark name/version, URL, model/effort, snapshot and empirical calibration. Each descriptor selects independent evidence dimensions; one score per dimension enters a bounded log-odds average before route-local outcome shrinkage. Uncalibrated/missing benchmarks do not change the general-intelligence prior or any qualification gate. Prompt-length estimates select the existing `prompt_token_threshold`/`above_threshold_*` fields (#413-compatible) when published; unknown prompt length conservatively uses the higher tier, and local spend from mismatched prompt-length buckets cannot replace that tier. The route audit records selected task benchmarks, descriptor and tier. Qualification, user overrides, role floors and quota reserve remain unchanged.
